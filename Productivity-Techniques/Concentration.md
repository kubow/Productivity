# Concentration Techniques

## Overview

Concentration is the ability to keep attention on one chosen task. Use these techniques for individual focus sessions; use [Deep Work & Focus](./Deep-Work-Focus.md) to design a broader system for protecting demanding work.

## Before You Start

- Define one visible outcome for the session.
- Reduce the task to a concrete next action.
- Remove unrelated tabs, devices, and notifications.
- Keep a capture note nearby for distracting thoughts.
- Choose a session length that matches your energy and the task.

## Pomodoro

1. Work on one task for 25 minutes.
2. Take a 5-minute break.
3. After four rounds, take a longer break.
4. Adjust the interval when the work needs a longer ramp-up; the timer serves the task, not the reverse.

[The Pomodoro Technique — Why It Works & How To Do It](https://todoist.com/productivity-methods/pomodoro-technique)




## Timeboxing

Give an activity a fixed start and finish. Decide in advance what “good enough” means, work until the box ends, and then deliberately continue, reschedule, delegate, or stop.

See [Time Blocking & Calendar Management](./Time-Blocking-Calendar-Management.md) for scheduling larger blocks across a day or week.

## When Attention Wanders

- Notice the distraction without arguing with it.
- Write it in the capture note if it needs attention later.
- Restate the next physical action and restart.
- If attention repeatedly fails, check task clarity, fatigue, hunger, stress, and environmental interruptions.
- Shorten the interval or take a real recovery break instead of forcing low-quality work.

Productivity methods can help many people, including people with ADHD, but they are not medical treatment. Persistent attention difficulties may warrant support from a qualified professional.
