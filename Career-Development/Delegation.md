# Delegation

## Overview

Delegation transfers responsibility for an outcome while the delegator retains appropriate accountability. Done well, it increases capacity, develops people, and places decisions closer to the relevant knowledge.

## Decide What to Delegate

Good candidates include work that:

- Develops someone’s relevant skills
- Can be owned with clear boundaries and feedback
- Benefits from expertise or context another person has
- Recurs and should not depend permanently on one person
- Frees the delegator to focus on responsibilities only they can perform

Do not casually delegate confidential matters, unresolved accountability, or a crisis without sufficient authority and support.

## Define the Delegation

Clarify:

- **Outcome:** What successful completion looks like
- **Reason:** Why it matters and who it affects
- **Scope:** What is included and excluded
- **Authority:** Which decisions the person may make independently
- **Constraints:** Deadline, budget, quality, policy, and risk boundaries
- **Resources:** Information, tools, access, people, and time available
- **Checkpoints:** When progress, risks, and decisions will be reviewed
- **Escalation:** What requires help or approval

Ask the person to restate the agreement. This tests shared understanding without turning the conversation into a quiz.

## Match Support to Readiness

- Give more direction when the task is new, consequential, or tightly constrained.
- Give more autonomy when the person has demonstrated relevant judgment.
- Separate coaching from taking the work back.
- Adjust checkpoints based on risk, not anxiety.

## Follow Through

- Make introductions and transfer access promptly.
- Be available at the agreed escalation points.
- Review outcomes against the original criteria.
- Give specific feedback and recognize the contributor.
- Document reusable lessons, templates, or decisions.

## Common Failure Modes

- Delegating a task without its purpose or decision authority
- Giving responsibility while retaining every decision
- Checking so frequently that ownership becomes impossible
- Disappearing until the deadline
- Taking work back after the first mistake
- Delegating only low-value administrative work and no growth opportunities
- Blaming the delegate for unclear expectations or missing resources
