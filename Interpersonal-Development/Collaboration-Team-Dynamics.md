# Collaboration and Team Dynamics

## Overview

Healthy collaboration combines a shared outcome, explicit responsibilities, reliable communication, psychological safety, and accountability. Teamwork problems often come from unclear agreements rather than poor intentions.

## Establish the Team Contract

Agree on:

- Purpose, intended outcomes, and current priorities
- Roles, decision rights, and areas of ownership
- Communication channels and expected response times
- Meeting cadence and criteria for calling a meeting
- How work, risks, and decisions will be documented
- How disagreement, feedback, and escalation will work
- What “done” means and how success will be measured

## Roles and Decisions

For important work, identify:

- **Owner:** accountable for moving the outcome forward
- **Contributors:** provide work or expertise
- **Decision-maker:** makes the call after appropriate input
- **People consulted:** contribute before the decision
- **People informed:** need the result and rationale

Avoid consensus by default. Use consensus when commitment from everyone is essential; otherwise clarify who decides and how input will be used.

## Psychological Safety

Teams learn faster when people can ask questions, admit mistakes, raise risks, and disagree respectfully without humiliation or retaliation. Leaders support this by inviting dissent, responding constructively to bad news, and acknowledging their own uncertainty.

Psychological safety does not mean low standards. Pair candor and learning with clear expectations and follow-through.

## Reliable Coordination

- Make requests explicit: owner, outcome, deadline, and constraints.
- Confirm commitments rather than assuming silence means agreement.
- Surface dependencies and blockers early.
- Record consequential decisions and why they were made.
- Prefer shared, visible work over private status updates.
- Close loops: acknowledge completion, changes, or missed commitments.

## Handle Tension Early

1. Describe the observable behavior or situation.
2. Explain its impact on the work or relationship.
3. Ask for the other person’s perspective.
4. Identify the need, constraint, or misunderstanding underneath.
5. Agree on a specific next behavior and follow-up point.

Use [Conflict Resolution](./Conflict-Resolution.md) when tension has escalated and [Feedback](./Feedback.md) for developmental conversations.

## Review Team Health

Periodically ask:

- Are priorities understood and stable enough to act on?
- Does each important outcome have one clear owner?
- Can people raise problems without undue risk?
- Are decisions timely and documented?
- Are meetings producing decisions and actions?
- Are workload and recognition distributed fairly?
- What should the team start, stop, and continue?
