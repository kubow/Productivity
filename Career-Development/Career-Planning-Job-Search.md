# Career Planning and Job Search

## Overview

Career planning is an iterative process: understand your direction, test possibilities, build evidence of relevant skills, and run a focused search. A useful plan creates experiments rather than pretending to predict an entire career.

## Take Stock

Review:

- Work you find meaningful or energizing
- Strengths you can demonstrate with evidence
- Skills you want to use or develop
- Values and conditions you will not compromise
- Preferred problems, people, pace, environment, and level of responsibility
- Financial, location, health, and family constraints

Create a short inventory of accomplishments, projects, feedback, metrics, and lessons. It will support résumés, interviews, reviews, and negotiation.

## Explore Directions

- Research roles through actual job descriptions, not titles alone.
- Speak with people doing the work and ask about normal weeks, tradeoffs, entry paths, and failure modes.
- Compare skill requirements with your evidence and gaps.
- Test uncertain directions through a project, course, shadowing opportunity, volunteer assignment, or short contract.
- Keep two or three plausible paths instead of demanding one perfect answer.

## Build a Search Strategy

1. Define target role families, organizations, and constraints.
2. Write a clear value proposition connecting your evidence to their problems.
3. Prepare a strong [résumé and cover letter](./Resume-Cover-Letters.md).
4. Build relationships before asking for referrals.
5. Use a small number of well-matched applications rather than indiscriminate volume.
6. Prepare accomplishment stories with the [Job Interview Guide](./Job-Interview.md).
7. Track applications, contacts, next actions, and follow-up dates.
8. Review results weekly and change the message, targets, or channels when evidence warrants it.

## Evaluate Opportunities

Consider role content, manager quality, team health, learning, autonomy, advancement, stability, compensation, flexibility, commute, workload, and organizational values. Separate must-haves from preferences before receiving an offer.

## Transition Well

- Confirm the offer and material terms in writing.
- Leave professionally and document handoffs.
- Set a 30/60/90-day learning plan.
- Clarify early expectations, stakeholders, decision rights, and feedback cadence.
