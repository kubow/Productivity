# Proactivity

## Overview

Proactivity means acting deliberately on what you can influence instead of waiting passively or reacting automatically. It includes initiative, foresight, ownership, and the judgment to involve others when action exceeds your authority.

## Circle of Control and Influence

Separate a situation into:

- **Control:** Your choices, preparation, attention, effort, communication, and boundaries
- **Influence:** Outcomes you can affect through requests, relationships, evidence, and example
- **Concern:** Conditions you can monitor but cannot directly control

Invest most effort in control and influence. This is not a reason to ignore structural problems; it helps identify the most useful next action and where collective action or escalation is needed.

## A Proactive Response

1. Pause before reacting.
2. Describe the situation without blame or prediction.
3. Identify the outcome, values, and people affected.
4. Separate controllable actions from assumptions and external constraints.
5. Choose the smallest useful next action.
6. Communicate ownership, dependencies, and risks.
7. Review the result and adjust.

## Initiative at Work

Before acting beyond normal responsibility, consider:

- Is the action aligned with team goals?
- Is it reversible and proportionate to the risk?
- Do I have the information, competence, and authority required?
- Who needs to be consulted or informed?
- Could the action create hidden work or interfere with another owner?
- What is the cost of waiting?

Proactivity is not constant urgency, unilateral decision-making, or accepting every problem as your personal responsibility.

## Build the Habit

- End meetings with owners and next actions.
- Raise risks with at least one possible response.
- Schedule preventive work before it becomes urgent.
- Replace “someone should” with a request, proposal, or explicit decision not to act.
- Reflect on one reactive moment each week and rehearse a better response.
