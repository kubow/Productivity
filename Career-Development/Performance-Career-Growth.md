# Performance and Career Growth

## Overview

Career growth comes from delivering useful outcomes, learning deliberately, building trusted relationships, and making your contribution visible without exaggeration.

## Clarify Good Performance

Ask your manager:

- What outcomes matter most this quarter?
- How will success be evaluated?
- Which responsibilities are mine to own?
- What tradeoffs should I make when priorities conflict?
- What would excellent performance at the next level look like?
- How and when should risks be raised?

Turn the answers into a small set of outcomes and review them regularly.

## Maintain an Accomplishment Record

Record outcomes, evidence, positive feedback, difficult problems, lessons, expanded responsibilities, and help given to others. Include context and impact, not only completed activities.

Use the record for one-to-ones, performance reviews, résumés, interviews, and promotion discussions.

## Use One-to-Ones Well

- Share progress against outcomes, not a list of every task.
- Raise decisions, risks, and blocked dependencies early.
- Ask for specific feedback and follow up on it.
- Discuss workload, development, relationships, and longer-term direction.
- End with clear actions and owners.

## Build a Development Plan

Choose one or two capabilities tied to real work. Define:

- The behavior or skill to improve
- Why it matters
- An assignment that provides practice
- A person who can observe and give feedback
- Evidence that would demonstrate progress
- A date to review and adjust the plan

## Prepare for Reviews and Promotion

- Understand the organization’s documented criteria and process.
- Demonstrate sustained next-level behaviors before focusing on title.
- Gather evidence from outcomes and stakeholders.
- Discuss gaps early; do not wait for the formal review.
- Ask what specific evidence is still missing and when the decision can be revisited.
- Treat compensation, title, and scope as related but distinct conversations.
