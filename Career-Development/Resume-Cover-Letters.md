# Résumé and Cover Letters

## Overview

Professional materials should make it easy for a reader to see the match between your evidence and a specific role. Accuracy, relevance, and clarity matter more than decoration.

## Build a Master Record

Maintain a private source document with roles, dates, responsibilities, projects, accomplishments, metrics, tools, recognition, education, and certifications. Tailor from this record instead of rewriting your history from memory.

## Résumé Structure

- Name and reliable contact information
- Optional concise professional summary when it adds context
- Relevant experience in reverse chronological order
- Selected skills supported by experience
- Education, certifications, projects, or publications where relevant

For each accomplishment, connect an action to its context and result:

> Improved **what** by doing **how**, resulting in **measurable or observable impact**.

Use honest scope and metrics. Do not invent precision or disclose confidential information.

## Tailor to the Role

1. Identify the role’s recurring outcomes and required capabilities.
2. Select evidence that demonstrates those capabilities.
3. Use the employer’s language where it accurately describes your experience.
4. Move the most relevant information earlier.
5. Remove details that consume attention without strengthening the match.

## Cover Letter

A useful cover letter explains:

- Why this role and organization make sense for you
- Which two or three pieces of evidence best predict success
- How your context connects to anything that may otherwise be unclear
- A concise, professional invitation to continue the conversation

Avoid repeating the résumé paragraph by paragraph.

## Quality Check

- Dates, titles, links, spelling, and contact details are correct.
- Claims are specific and defensible in an interview.
- Layout is readable in plain text and as a PDF.
- Important content is not hidden in graphics, headers, or footers.
- File name is professional and identifiable.
- Another person has reviewed it for clarity and errors.
