# Creating, Supporting & Maintaining Boundaries

*Source: DOAS² Dreaming On A Star 2 — [WWW.DOAS2.COM](https://www.doas2.com)*

## Overview

A guide to setting, communicating, and maintaining healthy boundaries in relationships and at work. Boundaries protect your well-being and clarify expectations with others.

---

## DO's

- **Clearly communicate your boundaries** — Make your limits explicit.
- **Be consistent** — Maintain your boundaries regularly.
- **Practice self-care** — Prioritize your well-being.
- **Respect others' boundaries** — Acknowledge and honor others' limits.
- **Seek support** — Engage with others for encouragement and accountability.

### How do I?

- **Communication:** Be clear and direct; use "I" statements to express needs and expectations.
- **Consistency:** Stick to and enforce your boundaries consistently.
- **Self-care:** Prioritize activities like exercise, meditation, or time with loved ones.
- **Mutual respect:** Others have boundaries too; avoid pushing them beyond their limits.
- **Support system:** Seek help from friends, family, or a therapist for accountability and encouragement.

---

## DON'Ts

- **Apologize for your boundaries** — Don't feel guilty for setting limits.
- **Ignore red flags** — Pay attention to warning signs of disrespect.
- **Over-explain your boundaries** — Avoid excessive justification.
- **Compromise your boundaries** — Don't give in for the sake of others or to avoid conflict.
- **Blame others for crossing your boundaries** — Take responsibility for communicating and enforcing your own boundaries.

### What not to do

- **Right to boundaries:** You have the right to set boundaries without guilt or apology.
- **Addressing disrespect:** If boundaries are consistently disrespected or you feel uncomfortable, address the issue and consider reevaluating the relationship.
- **Avoiding over-explanation:** Over-explaining can create confusion and opportunities for pushback.
- **Sticking to boundaries:** Do not compromise boundaries to please others or avoid conflict.
- **Responsibility:** Hold others accountable, but also take responsibility for communicating your boundaries clearly. Blaming others can lead to defensiveness.

---

## Why your boundaries are not working

- You apologize too much for setting them.
- You don't stick to them.
- You haven't verbalized them.
- You don't hold others accountable.
- You allow too much flexibility.
- You haven't identified what you need.

### What do I do?

1. **Clear and direct communication** — State what boundary was crossed and how it made you feel (e.g., *"When you showed up unannounced at my house, it made me feel uncomfortable because I need my privacy."*).
2. **Use "I" statements** — Express your feelings and needs rather than blaming (e.g., *"I need you to respect my boundaries and call before coming over."*).
3. **Be specific** — Clearly state unacceptable behaviors and what you need (e.g., *"I need you to respect my boundaries and not show up unannounced. Please call or text me before coming over."*).
4. **Listen to their response** — After expressing your needs, listen to the other person's perspective or for clarification.
5. **Set consequences** — If the person continues to cross boundaries, establish and enforce consequences (e.g., *"If you continue to show up unannounced, I will not answer the door or respond to your calls."*).
