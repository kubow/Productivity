# Problem-Solving

## Overview

Problem-solving turns an observed gap into a tested improvement. The aim is not merely to produce ideas, but to understand causes, choose a proportionate response, and learn from results.

## The Problem-Solving Cycle

1. **Describe the current state.** What happens, where, when, how often, and for whom?
2. **Define the desired state.** What observable outcome would count as improvement?
3. **Set scope and constraints.** Record deadlines, budget, authority, risks, and non-negotiables.
4. **Investigate causes.** Gather evidence before selecting a solution.
5. **Generate options.** Include prevention, simplification, automation, training, and doing nothing.
6. **Choose an experiment.** Prefer the smallest safe test that can distinguish between options.
7. **Implement with ownership.** Assign an owner, next action, due date, and feedback signal.
8. **Review the result.** Keep, adapt, or stop the intervention and document what was learned.

## Define the Problem Well

A useful statement avoids embedding a preferred solution:

> In context X, outcome Y differs from target Z by amount A, affecting B, since time C.

Distinguish:

- **Symptom:** What is visible
- **Impact:** Why it matters
- **Cause:** What produces or sustains it
- **Constraint:** What limits the response

## Root-Cause Tools

### Five Whys

Ask why repeatedly, validating each answer with evidence. Stop when further answers exceed the team’s control or cease to be useful. A Five Whys chain is a hypothesis, not proof.

### Cause-and-Effect Map

Explore possible causes across people, process, tools, information, incentives, environment, and external conditions.

### Process Mapping

Map inputs, decisions, handoffs, queues, rework, and outputs. Bottlenecks often become clearer when the real workflow is visible.

## Select an Option

Compare options by expected impact, effort, risk, reversibility, time to learn, and fit with constraints. Use a [decision matrix](./Decision-Making-Frameworks.md) when tradeoffs are complex.

## Avoid These Traps

- Solving the first explanation rather than the observed problem
- Optimizing one step while harming the whole system
- Adding a tool before clarifying the process
- Treating a people problem as only a training problem
- Launching a large solution without a feedback loop
- Failing to define who owns the next action
